# RIPPLE — Codex RPG Experience Overhaul v1.3

## Mission and authority
You are the lead game director, senior Phaser gameplay engineer, 2D technical artist, UI/UX designer and QA owner for Ripple. Work directly on the current repository. The current build is functional but does not yet feel like a real RPG: static actors, weak movement feedback, generic web-style HUD, flat map presentation and unclear gameplay flow. Your job is to deliver a cohesive, playable, polished cozy RPG vertical slice, not a new dashboard, not a design proposal and not another standalone HTML lab.

This prompt supersedes v1.2 only for presentation, actor simulation, dialogue, onboarding and polish. The handbook remains authoritative for domain rules, security, fixture coordinates, replay and economy. Ripple is the title; Lofi is the protagonist; Aurora is the companion. Use the existing branding, assets, audio v2 and fullscreen shell. Take inspiration from the readability, authored environments and game UX of Stardew Valley, Eastward and A Short Hike, without copying their art, layouts or code. Preserve the independent Ripple identity.

## 1. Game-first presentation and visual direction
The entire viewport belongs to the game. One persistent Phaser game and GameShell; React overlays are permitted. No navbar, permanent sidebar, dashboard cards, glassmorphism, gradient blobs, generic pill buttons, emoji icons, excessive rounded rectangles, decorative AI-generated copy or developer controls in player view. Do not copy developer labs. Build a small coherent RPG UI kit: deep navy outline, parchment/cream dialogue surfaces, glacier blue and restrained leaf green; consistent bevels, 9-slice frames, existing inventory icons and portraits. Use existing brand palette as source of truth, not new arbitrary colors. Use a legible rounded font with documented license or existing project font, consistent type scale, short labels and clear focus/pressed/disabled states. Avoid scaling up low-resolution art with blur. Keep decoration away from text. Use game art only where useful, not every panel covered in ornaments.

HUD: upper-left compact portrait + actual energy, level and XP where applicable; upper-right compact objective and optional minimap/pause; bottom contextual interact prompt, optional desktop hotbar. Keep at least the central play area clear. Hide irrelevant energy/XP in social scenes if not useful; no invented HP. No permanent funding or wallet panel. Bag is a slot grid with item icons, quantity, selected item description and relevant actions; NPC dialogue is an authored portrait panel; pause/settings are in-game menus. All bar tracks empty assets + runtime fill, no baked numbers. Use icon + short text when needed. Implement safe area, 44px minimum touch hit targets, keyboard focus, Escape/back, modal input lock and readable contrast. Mobile only: translucent circular draggable joystick at lower-left, contextual action + bag at lower-right, no duplicate controls. Portrait and landscape work; no page scrolling. Support fullscreen fallback and input mode override. No action behind modal.

Use existing source art to create genuinely distinct limb/stride frames where feasible, preserving silhouettes, palettes, perspective and foot anchors. Use state-specific poses/frames and short event-driven effects; prioritize walk/idle/build first. No constant bouncing while stationary, no sliding as a walk replacement. Animals: six interactive species need credible locomotion and species-specific reactions; five ambient species use appropriate movement (bird flutter, butterfly drift, fish swim, chicks/ducklings wander). Existing cardinal deterministic commands and energy cost remain unchanged. No movement while scene paused, no input stacking, clean animation/timer listeners on scene shutdown.

## 3. Living NPCs and animals
Use authored bounded patrols and activity waypoints, not random wandering across all tiles. Use walkable grid/A* or simple route lists, deterministic tie-breaking, per-actor speed, dwell, optional small seeded variation for social scenes only. Do not let actors enter water, overlap solids, block narrow crossings or block objective approaches. Keep player interaction reliable with dynamic actor location and a reachable adjacent approach; do not use stale spawn coordinates. When addressed, actor stops, faces player, finishes dialogue, then resumes route. Pause actor simulation during modal or scene transitions; prevent teleporting. If routes fail, recover to safe waypoint, never walk through collision. Do not alter authoritative expedition map or add random actors to scored replay collision.

Elder Toma walks around plaza and rests by monument; Piko moves between workshop and water lab; Nia visits animal sanctuary and plaza; Rumi works near recycler; Ari alternates farm plots and market; Banyu walks near dock and fishes; Mira tends market stall and nearby crates. Make them recognizable with short ambient actions, not always moving simultaneously. Preserve quest identities and reward rules. Biscuit wanders and plays fetch; Miso strolls, sits and chases a toy; Mochi/Cloud graze and enjoy gentle petting; Sunny/Puddle peck or waddle/follow briefly. Observe-only animals remain observe-only. Implement feed with one matching social food, +8 friendship, once per animal/day; play +5 once/day, no food cost, cap 100. Wrong food, repeat, out-of-range or insufficient inventory must not mutate state. Animations celebrate successful actions only, not trigger reward themselves. Fetch/chase/pet/follow can be short in-world scripted sequences (2–4 seconds), no new minigame engine needed. Feed/play remain optional, no punishment for absence, no real animal-care claims. Add moving water, subtle foliage sway, occasional wildlife, campfire and sparkles as inexpensive visual effects; do not animate every object or rely on heavy particles.
